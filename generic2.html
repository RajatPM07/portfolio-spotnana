Ria: AI underwriting for health insurance

A 3 hour manual review turned into a governed AI decision, without ever putting trust on credit.

1.1The problem, why, who, and validation

The problem
45 percent of monthly health insurance proposals were non standard, every one needed manual underwriting review.
Why it hurt
Each review took about 3 hours, so nearly half of incoming volume sat on the slowest path in the funnel.
Why now
A CXO level strategic priority, cutting across 8 teams and 5 integrated platforms.
Who for
The underwriters carrying the manual load, and every customer waiting on a 3 hour review.
Validation
Sole IC PM on the initiative, problem definition to working pilot in 2 months.

1.3How we earned the right to automate

Shipping an LLM feature is table stakes in 2026. The differentiating work is the governance around it. Ria is not full automation with a hidden safety net. It is a phased rollout behind human in the loop review gates, and review coverage only decreases after trust metrics clear an explicit bar.

Phase 1, complete

100 percent human review

Every AI verdict sat behind a human underwriter. This phase existed to generate evidence, not to save time.

Gate cleared, review coverage allowed to decrease
LLM to underwriter agreement: 95 percent, against a 92 percent bar Dangerous miss rate, AI more lenient than a human: targeted at near zero

The dangerous miss rate is the number I watch hardest, because it measures harm, not just accuracy.

Phase 2, live now

Risk tiered review

Lower risk tiers carry lighter review, high risk cases keep a full human pass. Coverage came down only because the gate above cleared.

Next gate, in active buildout, not yet live
Fairness disparity thresholds across groups Verdict to transcript traceability, every decision explainable back to its evidence
Phase 3, planned

Wider automation, only if earned

The next reduction in review coverage waits on the gate above. Both requirements are in buildout, and I would rather say that plainly than claim them early.

The broader frame: Ria sits inside a 7 domain governance framework: decision integrity, data protection and privacy, consent integrity, human oversight and accountability, security and abuse resistance, duty of care and interaction safety, and third party and vendor risk. PII masking and structured hallucination tracking are targets in buildout, not shipped claims, and the roadmap says so plainly. That honesty is the discipline I bring to whatever I ship next.

Elevate and the recommendation engine

A market share strategy on a 1,500 crore rupee portfolio, and the ML engine that turned it into a decision for every customer.

3.1The problem, why, who, and validation

The problem
A 1,500 crore health portfolio at 2.3 percent market share, growth gated on positioning gaps and an untapped existing customer base.
Why it hurt
Cross sell offers were accepted just 1.5 percent of the time. Nearly every offer shown was the wrong offer.
Why now
A competitive assessment made the positioning gaps concrete, and FY24-25 was the cycle to close them.
Who for
Existing policyholders who deserved a relevant next offer, and the segments addressed by new coverage architecture and pricing.
Validation
Authored the PRDs defining coverage architecture and pricing across segments. Mentored one Associate PM to independent ownership.

The engine is a production ML system shipped with an applied science team, partnering on feature engineering, evaluation metrics, and production guardrails. One model run predicts churn risk and cross sell propensity together, and the scores feed a 1 click cross sell surface inside checkout with a hard constraint from day one: zero cannibalization of the core purchase journey. It cleared that bar at an 18 percent click through rate and a 10 percent ARPU lift.

The transferable pattern: most personalization problems are next best action problems underneath. Propensity modeling, cohort targeting, and attach economics inside a checkout that must never degrade the core journey. That discipline travels to any product where a recommendation meets a transaction.
